# 🔁 The session loop

A coaching session is a loop with a fixed shape, and the shape is what turns practice into signal. Without it a session produces work that got done and nothing that says whether the person doing it is getting better.

## Six steps, and the order is the method

**choose level -> predict -> execute -> inspect -> improve -> teach back**

The order is not a suggested sequence, it is the instrument. A prediction recorded *after* the observation measures nothing -- it records agreement with a result already in hand, which is indistinguishable from understanding and is not the same thing. **Prediction is also the only step that cannot be recovered later.** Execution leaves artifacts, inspection can be redone against those artifacts, an improvement can be made tomorrow and a teach-back can be asked for at any point; a prediction that was never written before the run is gone. Skip it and the session produced practice but no signal.

Teach back is last for a related reason: it is the cheapest test of whether the improvement was understood or merely accepted, and it only means something once there is a concrete result to explain.

## "Choose level" means the difficulty of the exercise

**Level here is the level of challenge the learner is set** -- how hard the work chosen for this session is, relative to what they have already demonstrated. It is chosen first because everything downstream is calibrated against it: a prediction on work that is too easy is not diagnostic, and inspection of work that is far too hard cannot separate a gap in judgment from a gap in exposure.

**This is not the `L1`-`L5` operating level**, which is a run-state concept describing where attention sits during a piece of work and which carries a `descent_reason` and a `return_condition`. The two move independently -- a session set at a hard difficulty may still descend to `L1` mid-run when evidence goes weak, and that descent says nothing about the difficulty that was chosen. Keep the words apart: *difficulty* for the exercise, *operating level* for the run state.

## Ask rather than answer

**The default failure of coaching is answering.** The answer is faster, it is usually correct, and it ends the exchange -- which is exactly the problem, because the exchange is the product. Reach for a question first, and reach for it on reflex rather than after judging whether the person could get there alone; that judgment is itself something the question would have measured.

Every question has a right to a bad answer. **A confident wrong answer is more useful than a correct one supplied by the coach, because only the first is diagnostic** -- it locates the misconception precisely, which is the one thing a supplied answer can never do. A session that produced three wrong answers and one repair is worth more than one that produced four correct statements from the coach.

The questions themselves are subject matter, not method. The ones that work for agentic engineering are in [`Agentic_Engineering/07_COACHING.md`](../Agentic_Engineering/07_COACHING.md).

## Prefer progressively harder real work over exercises

**Reading is not the instrument, and a synthetic problem cannot produce the failure modes that teach.** A constructed exercise has a known answer and a bounded surface, which means the two things most worth learning -- what goes wrong that nobody anticipated, and what to do once it has -- are designed out of it before it starts. Real work supplies them for free.

This is also what makes the difficulty choice in step one meaningful: raising difficulty means picking harder *real* work, not adding artificial constraints to easy work. The ordered ladder of what to hand someone next, for this discipline, is the practice progression in [`Agentic_Engineering/07_COACHING.md`](../Agentic_Engineering/07_COACHING.md).
