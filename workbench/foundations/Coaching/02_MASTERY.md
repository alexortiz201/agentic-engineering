# 🏅 Mastery, and what a session leaves behind

A session that cannot be scored and leaves nothing behind is indistinguishable from work that happened to get done. Scoring says where the person is; the record says what the next session builds on. Both are written from evidence, and neither is written from impression.

## Score a named capability 0-4, from recorded evidence only

| | |
|---|---|
| 0 | unfamiliar |
| 1 | explains it |
| 2 | applies it with guidance |
| 3 | operates and **diagnoses** it independently |
| 4 | transfers or improves a proven primitive |

**The gap between 2 and 3 is diagnosis.** Operating something without being able to say why it failed is a 2, however smoothly the operating went -- the whole distinction is whether the person can work backwards from a failure to its cause without being handed the thread. It is the most commonly overstated step on this scale, because the two look identical on any run that succeeds.

The score attaches to a **named** capability, not to a person in general. "Good at this" is not a score; "operates a bounded repair loop independently, diagnoses its exits" is.

## Do not inflate

Each of these is a way a score gets manufactured rather than observed, and all of them are refusals:

- **Never invent a historical score.** A level that was never assessed is absent, not zero and not inferred backwards from where the person is now.
- **Never infer mastery from a fluent explanation**, or from an artifact existing. Fluency is a 1. An artifact proves something was produced, not that its producer could diagnose it.
- **Never average away a weak dimension.** Weak verification or unsafe authority does not get balanced out by strength elsewhere -- **one unsafe authority decision is not offset by four sound ones**, because the failure it admits is not reduced by the decisions that went well.

The pressure in all three directions is the same: a higher score is more pleasant to record and reads as progress. It is also the thing that makes the next difficulty choice wrong, which is how an inflated score is eventually paid for.

## What persists

A session that leaves nothing behind cannot be built on. Record:

- decisions and predictions
- the difficulty level worked at, the mastery scores it produced, and the recorded evidence behind any movement in either -- naming which of the two moved, since they move independently
- artifacts produced
- failures and their root causes
- **a reusable lesson, or the reason not to extract one**
- measured time, cost and interventions
- demonstrated change in mastery
- exactly one next exercise

The fifth is the one most often skipped, and **"nothing worth extracting yet" is a complete and legitimate answer** -- recording it is what keeps reuse earned rather than assumed, and it is the difference between a session that declined to generalize and one that never considered it.

**Exactly one next exercise**, not a list. A list defers the choice of difficulty to whoever opens the record next, and that choice is the first step of the next session precisely because it needs the evidence this one just produced.
